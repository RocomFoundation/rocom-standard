Robots, people and buildings. Working together in healthcare.

ROCOM is an open standard for connecting robots, building systems, workforce platforms and task systems — with clear responsibilities and testable interfaces.

One practical example

A hospital needs to deliver supplies from a central store to a ward on another floor. A delivery robot is assigned the task by the hospital's task system. Before leaving, the robot requests door access from the building management system — and receives authorisation for a defined time window.

The robot reaches the lift. It sends a dispatch request to the BMS. The lift is currently serving human traffic, so the BMS returns a queued response with an estimated arrival. The robot waits at a safe stand-off distance.

The lift becomes available. The BMS dispatches it. The robot boards, rides to the target floor, and exits. At the ward entrance, the robot re-requests zone authorisation because the previous door access has expired. The BMS grants it, and the robot proceeds to the delivery point.

At each step, the robot system executes the movement, the BMS controls building resources, and the task system tracks progress. No single system takes over the others' authority.

This is an illustrative workflow. ROCOM specifies the interfaces and responsibilities — it does not execute them. Local robot safety, building systems and required human decisions remain authoritative.

Security at the connections

Each system has its own responsibilities. Their connections need clear rules too.

ROCOM defines who may request an action, what data may be shared, and how systems respond when access is denied or a dependency fails.

Four domains, one shared standard

Healthcare operations involve different systems with different expertise. ROCOM defines how they connect.

Robot

Delivery, transport and support robots connect to healthcare workflows through shared identity, capability and task interfaces — while retaining the specialist capabilities of their fleet-management systems.

Infrastructure

Doors, lifts and shared building resources are coordinated through building management and access-control systems, with clear responsibilities, capacity limits and permissions.

Workforce

Staffing and availability systems coordinate human teams and robot capacity, respecting the distinct skills, responsibilities and operating constraints of each.

Task

Clinical and operational task systems share priorities, progress and completion evidence across participating systems, with traceability at every step.
